# Mag Locks, REX & Basic Egress Wiring

A magnetic lock holds a door shut **with electricity**. That single fact is why this
course is a hard safety gate: get the release wrong and people are trapped. Across the
sections below you learn the two locks and their opposite rules, the wiring and the
armature seal that separate a pro from a callback, how people get out, how to follow the
power to a fault, and how to prove the work the Redex way.

## Two locks, opposite rules

A store needs its back door locked after hours. You've got a mag lock and an electric
strike in the truck. Pick one — then see if people can still get out in a fire.

<Sim spec="ac-203/mag-vs-strike-pick" />

A magnetic lock and an electric strike chase the same goal — keep the door shut — by
doing **opposite things with power:**

- **Mag lock = powered to LOCK.** Energize it and the magnet holds. Cut the power and it
  _releases._ That's **fail-safe**, and it's wired **normally closed (NC)**.
- **Electric strike = powered to UNLOCK.** It stays locked until you signal it. That's
  fail-locked (fail-secure), wired **normally open (NO)** — a different course.

This course is about the mag — **the one that holds the door shut _with electricity,_**
which is exactly why it's dangerous. Get the release wrong and people are trapped. That's
why this is a hard safety gate.

<Callout tone="safety" label="Safety">
  Free **egress** is non-negotiable. You may control who comes _in_ as tightly as you like — you may
  never stop someone getting _out._
</Callout>

## No power, no hold — and the seal that makes it real

**The Redex mag-lock wiring (the 1200S):**

- Lock **Black → 24V** on the power supply.
- Lock **Red → NC** on the Alarm.com controller. Locked = **24V** through the controller;
  unlocked = **0V** (the relay opens). Lose the 24V supply and the mag drops — **fail-safe.**

Now the part nobody writes down — **the armature seal.** A 1200S only holds its
**1,200 lb** when the armature plate seats **dead flush** against the magnet face.
Magnetic hold falls off fast with the tiniest air gap or tilt. So you mount the armature
**secured but floating:**

- **Loctite** the bolt so it _never_ vibrates loose over thousands of cycles.
- But **never torque it solid.** Use the **rubber washers from the kit** between the
  armature and the frame — at least one as standard, more for spacing — so the plate can
  **rock** (twist slightly any direction) and self-align to a 100% seal when the door
  closes.

<Callout tone="warning" label="Warning">
  Over-tighten the armature and you get the worst failure there is: the mag _reads_ locked but only
  half-seals, holds a fraction of its rating, and the door pulls open — and **nothing on the panel
  tells you.**
</Callout>

Set the washers and torque on the bench: over-tight gives a weak hold, loose fails later.

## How people get out

A compliant mag door has **two ways out**, and the tech wires both:

1. **Power loss** — lose the 24V supply, the mag drops. (Built in by the wiring above.)
2. **The push-to-exit (TS9)** — the always-works manual release. Wire it **in-line in the
   mag's power** — the power runs _through_ the button before the mag. Press it and you
   cut power directly to the lock; it releases no matter what the controller is doing.

**REX vs. the button — know the difference:**

- The **REX (Bosch DS160) → the controller Inputs** (two available). A **REX** _signals
  the controller_ to release.
- The **push-to-exit cuts power directly.** A motion REX alone is **not** a
  code-compliant release — the manual button is required.

**Placement is code (NFPA 101 §7.2.1.6.2):** the **push-to-exit** mounted **40–48 in.**,
**within 5 ft** of the door, releases for **≥30 s**, and signed. Place it in the live
envelope below — the wrong height or distance flags why.

<Sim spec="ac-203/button-placement" />

**The reader is OSDP (ET-35).** Redex runs OSDP, not Wiegand — 4-conductor RS-485,
**AES-128 encrypted, supervised** (the panel knows if the reader drops or is tampered),
bidirectional, good to 1,000 ft. Wiegand is unencrypted, one-way, ~500 ft.

<Callout tone="warning" label="Escalate">
  If the building has a **fire-alarm system**, the fire-alarm release interface is a **specialist
  job** — flag it and escalate. Don't wire it on this install.
</Callout>

## Follow the power

Dead mag? **Don't guess — follow the power** with a meter, in order:

1. **The 24V supply** — is `24V` coming out? No → it's the supply.
2. **The controller** — `24V` through it when locked, `0V` when unlocked? That proves the
   controller is passing power correctly.
3. **End of the run** — meter red/black at the lock end. Lost your `24V` here but had it at
   the controller? You've got a **nick or a cut** in the wire.
4. **At the mag** — `24V` all the way to the lock and it still won't hold? _Now_ you can
   suspect the mag.

<Callout tone="info" label="Note">
  Most "dead mag" calls trace back to **the push-button wiring**, not the mag. And a brand-new mag
  being bad is about `one in a million` — chase the wire first.
</Callout>

A dead door and a virtual voltmeter: trace it to the fault.

## Decide: is this egress install code-compliant?

This is the flagship branching scenario — you continue to it as a separate sim, not one
embedded here. You commission a mag-lock **egress** door through a sequence of real
decisions: fail-state, motion-REX sufficiency, **push-to-exit** placement, and release
time. Then **the emergency hits and people try to get out.**

The emergency is a **power loss**, and the manual **push-to-exit** must hold the door
released for the full **≥30 s** while people clear. A wrong call **traps them** — the
button too high, a motion REX the person never trips, or a door that won't drop. The
right build lets everyone out. You don't read about the consequence. You watch it.

## Do one, and prove the seal

In the build sim — a separate unit, not embedded here — you wire the full mag + **REX** +
**push-to-exit** on the 3D Virtual Door and prove **both** release paths live: supply loss
and the button.

Then the install isn't done until you **prove the seal — the Redex pull test:** lock the
door, **go outside, and pull as hard as you can.** A properly seated 1,200 lb hold doesn't
budge. If it gives, your armature isn't sealing — back to the washers.

Remember the fire-alarm interface is a **specialist job** that is **escalated**, not wired
on this install.
